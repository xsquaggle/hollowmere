# Hollowmere: decisions

Newest first. Each entry records what was decided and why, so later changes don't undo it by accident.

## 2026-10-06

- **The story pass, and the end of chapter one (build 31).** Every letter, logbook page, lore line and thing people say, read in the order you meet them, then tied together, and an ending for chapter one: supper on Lantern Row. Lawson was asked how chapter one should end (supper with the uncle only hinted at, supper with him there, or a quiet letter) and hadn't answered when this shipped, so it went with the recommended one: the uncle is only hinted at.
  - The read-through fixed the timeline. The town went under in 1966, sixty years ago: Pell's "fifty-odd years late" is now sixty, your uncle looked for the way under for forty years (the banquet said fifty), Ottilie rang her ferry bell for fifty (it said forty), and Old Gristle was fed for thirty, so "forty" stays the uncle's number. Ottilie's confession now says she was crossing home from the Row's Saturday supper, which the rest of the story builds on. Barnaby's boat shop counts the coast's fish instead of saying six (there are 13).
  - The story script: `npm run story` writes `docs/STORY.md`, every line from the content tables in about the order you meet them (the opening, the coach and Grey are in game code and aren't in it). `npm run check` and `npm test` fail if it's stale, so it always matches the game.
  - Ottilie's lines follow the story. Her idle lines are by chapter (each water you open, then the supper): the newest chapter's most, the earlier ones now and then, and the weather's when it has something to say. A line can stop once it's no longer true (the Ash Caster one, once you own it), or wait until it is (the Mayor's toffees, once you've caught him). She remarks once on a Legendary or rarer you've just caught at the lake, and the Mayor and the Calf by name, and now and then on the rod in your hand. She calls you kid until the day she tells you about the flood, and Keeper after.
  - Wren's corkboard gains a note each for the Quarter, the Bonewhistle, the Hollow and the supper, and her theories end up unsettlingly right. Wren and Pell each add lines once you've been further (the Quarter, the Hollow, the supper).
  - Every fish remembers something of the old town: a second line on its journal page once you've caught enough of it (10 up to Rare, 3 Epic, 2 Legendary, the first for anything rarer: `MASTERY.memory`). The catch card says when a new one is in the journal (for a fish rarer than Legendary it comes with the first catch, which the card already marks as new).
  - Pell's round has a last step, Supper on the Row, and nothing of it shows until your uncle's last page (the Stillwater Mirror's) is read. Then he has news: the Row's invitation, postmarked 1966, which opens as you take it. Its how-to sits under his ask. Once you've been, he pays 25 Glimmer and a letter of his own comes in the same post, which he pockets unopened.
  - The supper comes in the Drowned Quarter while the tower bell rings by itself (3:12 to 4:12) with the Stillwater Mirror in your hand. Another rod gets the coach's word once a game day. A float that's only waiting comes in first. The reflection turns the right way up, and the whole Row is at one long table under lanterns and bunting: Edith and Walter Crane, Harold Dunmore the baker, Ivy Hale, Hattie, the Mayor, the Postmaster, and Albert and Josephine Finch, each line paying off a letter or a bottle you've read. The camera leans toward whoever's speaking, and their face is on the line's card. One place at the far end is laid with a thermos by the plate, and its chair is pushed back. Walter comes down the street late ("the water was up past the second step"), and he and Edith dance in the street under the lanterns. Then the bell stops, the windows go out one by one, and it's a reflection again. The end-of-chapter card follows, with a tally (the day, fish met, letters posted, logbook pages), then the fishing goes on. The bell stays quiet for the rest of that hour. The supper is kept once the bell stops, so one cut short (the app closed partway) comes round again, and one closed before its card shows the card on its own next time. Back, Escape or a swipe skips to the card, and from the card goes back to the water; nothing else (a note, a tip, a catch's news) opens over the supper or the opening. Settings has "Watch the supper again" after, under About (the App tab, called Install until the game is installed). It wants nothing on the line (reel in first) and changes nothing, though the clock runs on while you watch, as it does the first time.
  - Walter and the dance were first on the house side of the table, which put them off the left edge of a phone held upright. They're now out in the open street past the far end, framed by the tower, where every screen sees them over everyone's heads. With reduced motion the camera cuts instead of drifting, and the dance holds still.
  - Speed: the scene first ran at about 20 frames a second in the test browser on a phone-sized screen. The game's own scene stops drawing under it, the folk at the table are drawn once and kept until they blink or move, the sky goes under the rest as one picture, and every window's glow is drawn into it (drawn live only once the windows start to go out). It now runs at about 60 (the game's own speed) on a phone-sized screen, and about 25 at 1280 by 800, where the game itself runs about 30 in the same browser. A painted layer is capped at about 7 million pixels.
  - Playtest > Tools > The story: ready the supper (Pell's round, the last page, the invitation, the Mirror), row to the Quarter at 3:12 with the Mirror, watch it, forget it, or make Ottilie say something now. The Quarter's tools skip the supper step.
  - Checked at 390 by 844, 820 by 1180, 1280 by 800 and 844 by 390, after the Drowned Quarter fix (PR #12) showed a scene can break only on wide screens. The review found the end card ran off a phone held sideways (the button below the screen, and no way out): it now scrolls if it must, and on a short screen the title and lines are smaller and the tally is one row. The scene's pictures (about 26 MB on a phone, 70 on a laptop) are let go when it closes.
  - The review's story notes: your uncle only found the way under at the very end (his logbook spans his last year and a half), so Ottilie's confession and the Mothmouth's lore now say so; Wren crossed over the street on the ferry rather than rowing; Pell was sent to bed early for being cheeky to the Mayor, which is why the other children stay up; spellings are British throughout (harbour, neighbours) and every apostrophe is curly. The story script now has Wren's Quarter lines, the lines people say when you give back what they lost, logbook page 3 with the Quarter, and Pell's last step with the supper.

- **The Hollow (build 30).** The sixth water, and the one the story has been pointing at since page 1: a cave under Stillwater Lake, under your uncle's shack, where something asleep dreams the fish. With it come the deferred Godly pieces: the first Godly fish (the Sleeper's Scale), the Ledger, omens and falling stars, and the uncle's own rod, the Stillwater Mirror.
  - The way down, a page at a time. Page 3 comes in the next bottle once the Quarter is open (and page 2 read), wrapped round your uncle's map: it makes the map you have whole, or starts one, whole, where you are, and its cache holds page 4. The first pass put page 4 in the cache of the next map you pieced together, which waited on three map pieces: about 1 h 50 in the simulator, and over 5 hours in one run. With his map it's a few minutes. Page 5 is in Grey's beak on the lake dock once page 4 is read and you've heard the tower ring at 3:12 in the Quarter: tap him for it.
  - Page 5 says to ring the tower's hand bell from the dock at 3:12. With the Drowned Bell in a vest pocket it hangs from an iron arm on the dock's left pile. Rung while the tower rings by itself (3:12 to 4:12), the tower answers from under the water, the lake goes still, and the water drains from under the shack for a real minute, so the trapdoor opens (its fix-up line now says the water holds it shut, not that it needs a key). Climb down, and the Hollow stays open through the trapdoor or on the map ("Climb down"). Rung at any other hour nothing answers, and under the Stopped Pocket Watch the lake can't.
  - The dark is the Hollow's mechanic. It's dark day and night (no sky, no weather) but for the lantern's pool by the ledge, the glowing spore shelf under the far wall, and from 10 to 2 a shaft of lake light through a crack in the roof. Most fish need light to bite: one that reaches your float in the dark follows it instead (you see two pale glints), and drifts off after 9 seconds with the float still (a twitch buys 2.5 more). Hold the line and the float draws in toward the lantern's pool, the fish following, until it's in the light and bites as any fish would. The Eyeless Koi bites in the dark (it swims toward sound), and so do the fish that glow. Tap the lantern to put it out. The Lantern Rod's lamp lights the water round its float down here too. Grey is there the first time you climb down, and about one visit in three after.
  - Ten fish, six of which count toward the water: the Lampless Perch (common, the lantern's pool), the Spore Loach (common, glows, the shelf), the Echo Bream (uncommon, the drip line), the Keyhole Eel (uncommon, round the eye), the Eyeless Koi (rare, bites in the dark) and the Mothmouth (Legendary, out in the far dark). The extras: the Looking-Glass Carp (Epic, in the noon shaft, 1 cast in 12 there), the Inkling (Exotic, secret, only for a cursed rod: 1 in 45 with the Bonewhistle), the Drowned Moon (Mythic, glows, new-moon nights out in the dark, 1 in 90) and the Sleeper's Scale (Godly). They go in the freshwater tank, but for the Drowned Moon and the Scale, which no tank holds. The Carp was 1 in 6 and 700 coins at first, which made it a quarter of noon catches and the noon shaft 91,000 coins an hour; at 1 in 12 and 600 it's about 51,000.
  - The eye: a sink in the cave floor at the deep spot. At 3:12, with every light out (the lantern on its hook and the Lantern Rod's lamp), once every fish the Hollow counts is caught, it opens; before that it only stirs, and a tip says so the first time. The first cast into the open eye brings up the Scale. After that it bites there at the Godly rate (1 in 50,000), which in practice means once.
  - Godly: when one's hooked everything stops for a second (less with reduced motion) and the drips hang where they are; at the landing light comes down through the roof onto it; its card has a double gold border with slow rays, and its name is inked in as a Mythic's is. The Scale can't be sold, tanked or mounted: the card says Priceless, and its one button is Into the Ledger, which writes the catch (the day, the time to the minute, the water, the weather, the size) on its journal page. Then the eye closes, the Hollow stays lit for the rest of that in-game day, and up out of where the eye was comes the Stillwater Mirror, with page 6, the uncle's last.
  - The Stillwater Mirror: a quest rod a step past the Deepwater Caster (line 2.1, reel 1.55, luck .9, value 1.8, three rune sockets). On a clear night, outdoors, nine stars show in the water (in new places each night, always where a cast reaches); a float on one makes Exotic and rarer bites three times as likely. Lawson chose this perk on Oct 6, over choosing the sky or always glassy water.
  - Omens: every 2 to 3⅓ hours of play (never in the tutorial or before 40 catches), for 3 real minutes, an omen settles over the water you're on. It's never put into words: the water goes glassy, no birds come over and a gull about to drop doesn't, the place's sounds fall away but the bell at 3:12, the colours turn a shade grey and violet, and in the Hollow the drips stop. Mythic and Godly bites there are five times as likely, and the Bonuses page doesn't show it.
  - Falling stars: on a clear night outdoors, every 1½ to 4 minutes of such a night, a star comes down into the water where a cast can reach and leaves a zone of starlight for 15 seconds, where Exotic and rarer bites are three times as likely (a ring and a label count it down). One lucky moment at a time: no star falls while a gull's lucky splash is up or a gull is about to drop, and no gull drops while a star's zone is up.
  - Ottilie tells you about the night the town went under, once, the next time you see her after the Hollow opens.
  - What it pays (the simulator, steady player, Deepwater Caster, coins an hour at 8 PM): the lantern's pool 9,800, the shelf 7,000, open water 23,800, the drip line 30,000, the far dark 38,600, the eye's deep water 53,900; open water from 10 to 2, in the shaft, 51,200. So the Hollow pays a little better than the Quarter (about 30,000 to 35,000) for the time it costs to lead fish in, and the coast's trench is still the best water.
  - The simulator knows the Hollow: its spots, the lights, and the time to draw a fish in from the dark to the edge of the lantern's pool, where its fight starts (under the Lantern Rod's own lamp there's no draw). A midday stretch on open water is in the shaft four hours in nine. Not the eye, omens or falling stars (they come too seldom to move an hour's coins). The whole-run report follows the way down: pages 1 and 2 in the 3rd and 7th bottles, page 3 in the next once the Quarter's open, page 4 a few minutes later, page 5 once it's been in the Quarter at 3:12 (with page 4 read it rows out for it), the Drowned Bell from the deep pool in foggy small hours (the weather rolled as the game rolls it), then the next 3:12. Once down, every other stretch goes after a Hollow fish still missing, and the run goes on past everything bought until all six are caught.
  - The whole run now (21 runs, steady, after the review's fixes): the Quarter 4 h 06, page 3 4 h 54, the Hollow 5 h 59 (single runs 3 h 32 to 11 h 11), every Hollow fish 6 h 47, Deepwater 8 h 26, everything bought about 10.5 hours (median 10 h 25, single runs 9 h 31 to 12 h 33). The Drowned Bell often turns up by chance at the deep pool long before it's needed (median 2 h 00); when it doesn't, it's the long wait, as fog in the small hours comes about one night in three. The slowest runs wait on bottles for pages 1 and 2. Smaller sets gave medians from 9 h 47 (7 runs) to 11 h 05 (11 runs), so one small set says little. data/pace.js is regenerated, with the way down in it.
  - Playtest's Hollow menu hands over pages 3 to 5 and the Drowned Bell, sets the clock to 3:12, drains the lake, opens the Hollow, readies the eye, starts an omen, drops a falling star, and gives the Mirror.
  - Edge cases settled in review. The Hungry Hook never eats a Godly fish, and Echo never brings one back (it would have skipped the eye). The Mirror comes up once the card is closed and nothing else is on screen, so a quick cast can't hide it. Page 3's bottle shows only the page, so a tip tells you about the map folded into it. With the Stopped Pocket Watch holding the clock at 3:12 the eye stays open while it holds; it still gives the Scale only once. The Wet Almanac says no weather reaches the Hollow and keeps the moon. The Twin Spool's second float, out in the dark, only gets the fish that bite without light. The Bonuses page shows the Mirror's stars outdoors only.

- **The Drowned Quarter (build 29).** The fifth water: the half of old Hollowmere that went under the lake, Lantern Row, the post office and the bell tower, reached in Pell's old post-office rowboat. Pell becomes a character with a round of his own, and the two story rods come with it: the Tidecaller and the cursed Bonewhistle, with Dread.
  - The way in: once you have the boat, Pell asks you to fix the post office's rowboat, for 8,000 coins (his sheet opens from the mail boat, from his boat in the Quarter, or from the map's Drowned Quarter card). Priced so the Quarter opens a little over an hour after the boat; in the simulator it opens at 3 h 27, between the Saltline and the Gale Rod. It's on the map at the lake's west end, a short row out ("Row out").
  - Casting through the street is the Quarter's mechanic. Every building is a solid with openings in it. A cast that would come down behind a wall hits it (Tok!, Clang! on a lamp post, Thunk! on Pell's boat) and drops into the street at its foot; the aim says so first. One aimed at a door or a window goes in, and the float sits in the drowned room, seen through the opening; a fish hooked in there comes out under the sill to fight. Six spots, drawn rather than marked: Lantern Row (the street), the doorways, the front rooms, the post office, the bell tower (deep) and the square past it (far).
  - Pell's round, in order: fix the rowboat; post Edith's letter (the one he never could deliver) through No. 4's door, by clipping it to your line on his sheet and casting it in (the aim reads POST IT at the right door); land a Postman Sturgeon, which brings her answer; post Albert's letter into the bell tower; post the three letters the post office never sent. Rewards: 600 coins, 8 Glimmer, 1,200 coins, then the Tidecaller, his father's rod. Every posted letter's answer comes back on a later Postman Sturgeon and Pell reads it out (five answers, addressed to people still in town). Albert's letter can come up at the lake as before; it and the three unsent letters also drift out of the post office window among the drowned pages, as envelopes, one at a time once his round reaches them, so no step waits on treasure luck. In the simulator the round takes about 50 minutes from the rowboat, the Tidecaller at 4 h 15.
  - Pell is reached in his boat at the post office's corner in the Quarter (moved there from in front of its big window, which it blocked), on his mail boat at your dock, and he calls in at the dock on his own when he has something for you, then waits a little while to be tapped.
  - Nine new fish, as the marsh and the river got more than the plan's four: the Sooty Gudgeon and the Parlour Roach (commons), the Hingejaw in the doorways, the Drainpipe Eel (rain), the Lace Shad (fog, rare), the Postman Sturgeon (rare, the post office, three times likelier from 6 to 11 AM), the Hearthfish (Epic, at night, only on a lit window's reflection in the street, 1 cast in 7 there), the Paper Carp (Epic, secret: 1 cast in 5 with a drowned page on the hook) and the Choir Fish (Legendary, at the bell tower while its bell rings, 1 cast in 6). They live in the freshwater tank: the Quarter is under the lake.
  - Drowned pages float out of the post office every 35 to 70 seconds and drift up the street; tap one and it's on your hook for the next cast. At night the windows and the lamps are lit, but only in the water.
  - The bell tower's clock stopped at 3:12, and every night from 3:12 to 4:12 its bell rings (a real minute). With the Drowned Bell in a pocket you can ring it yourself from the rowboat, once every six in-game hours: the "tower" combo, live now. While it rings the tower's fish bite 40% sooner and the Choir Fish answers.
  - The Tidecaller: a quest rod between the Saltline and the Gale Rod (line 1.7, reel 1.35, luck .55, value 1.5, three rune sockets). Tap the conch on a cord at its butt and the rain comes in where you are for two in-game hours (over about half a minute, and out again), once a day, or every twelve in-game hours with the Wet Almanac in a pocket: the "tide" combo, live now. It can't call rain while it's raining, while Playtest has the weather pinned, or while the Stopped Pocket Watch holds the clock (the rain would never arrive); the hand bell won't ring the tower then either.
  - The Bonewhistle: the first cast that goes in at the bell tower's door while the bell rings brings it up instead of a fish, as loot of its own (Something came up out of the tower). Its numbers are the Saltline's, and it's cursed: a hooked fish comes straight in with no fight (about three seconds of reeling, never a lost fish), and catches can come up Inked (1 in 12, worth five times as much and 3 Glimmer), the mutation that waited for cursed gear.
  - Dread, 0 to 100, shown as an eye under the coins that opens as it rises: every catch with the Bonewhistle adds 3 (common) to 40 (Godly), 2 more if Inked. It fades by 8 an in-game hour while you hold another rod. It lends the Bonewhistle luck, up to +60 at 100. At 75 you're warned; at 100 the lake looks back: an eye opens in the water and takes the coins the Bonewhistle's fish sold for since Dread was last empty (never more than you have) and its fish still in your keepnet, and Dread is empty again. An Inked catch haunts the next cast: an ink shadow comes to the float first, circles it and sinks away if you leave it; twitch at it and it adds 6 Dread and the cast is over.
  - What it pays (the simulator, steady player, coins an hour, Deepwater Caster, noon): the street 24,100, the doorways 27,500, the front rooms 22,600, the post office 33,800 (49,700 in the morning, the Sturgeon's round), the tower 32,300, the square 22,700; at night the street with its reflections 44,800; the tower while the bell rings 189,000, for its one minute. Every spot at noon about 27,000 against the coast's 31,400, with the coast's trench (65,000 by day, 89,000 at night) still the best water. The Brasscap pays about 18,400 there. The Bonewhistle at Dread 50 about 24,600. The Quarter's fish values were raised to get there (the Sturgeon to 150, the Choir Fish to 1,600), and the Choir Fish's chance cut from 1 in 4 to 1 in 6.
  - The simulator knows the Quarter: its spots, a page on the hook as often as pages drift by and the player scoops them (`SIM_PLAYERS` pages .8), casts at night onto the lit reflections (refl .5), the bell with the clock, and the Bonewhistle's Dread held where it's named (`--dread`, and a Dread setting on the Balance tab). Walls aren't modeled: every cast goes where it's aimed. The whole-run report fixes the rowboat once it has the boat, follows Pell's round (every other stretch in the Quarter while it's under way) and never uses the Bonewhistle. Its hours come in buckets that miss 3:12, so it never hears the bell or lands a Choir Fish.
  - The whole run now (11 runs, steady): the boat 1 h 52, the Quarter 3 h 27, the Tidecaller 4 h 15, Gale 5 h 07, Deepwater 7 h 48, everything about 10.5 hours (median 10 h 26, single runs 8 h 15 to 11 h 34; 9 h 43 in a set of 7). data/pace.js is regenerated, with the rowboat in it.
  - Playtest's Quarter menu opens it, does Pell's next step, rings the bell, sends a page or an envelope, gives the Bonewhistle, sets Dread, haunts the next cast, and readies the conch.

- **Gullrock Coast finished (build 28).** The plan's "the rest of its fish and spots": two new spots, two new things the sea does, and four new fish, taking the coast from 9 fish to 13. No new character.
  - The seventh wave: every seventh swell is a big one (`SWELL.set`), so one comes about every 52 seconds. For 2.4 seconds before it rises the far water darkens and a warning comes (a tip the first time, then news). It hits a line 1.7 times harder than a swell, washes out a strip half as wide again, and its gauge reads SEVENTH WAVE. Behind it the water is churned for 5 seconds: a float waiting there gets a fish at once, and 1 churned cast in 16 is the Comber Tarpon. The first pass had it 1 in 4, which made the Tarpon about 5% of coast catches, more than the trench's Gaslight Angler at night, and paid an Epic every 20 casts; at 1 in 16 it's about 1.4% of catches in every coast spot but the trench (where it never bites). Playtest has a "Seventh wave next" button.
  - The wash: a swell breaking on a sea stack leaves white water round its foot for 4.2 seconds (longer after a seventh wave). Cast into it and the wait for a bite is 35% shorter, and the Spindrift Bass (uncommon) hunts there and nowhere else. A cast landing in the wash keeps that spot for the cast, as the wait was already set. Swells can't wash out a cast in the wash: you cast there just after one has passed.
  - The wreck: the Marigold (Barnaby's galley burner came off her) lies past the stacks, well out (0.77 of the way to the horizon, which every sea rod reaches). Treasure is 60% likelier there, so a haul comes about 1 cast in 8 against 1 in 12 elsewhere, and the Wreck Conger (rare) lives there and nowhere else, 2.2 times likelier at night. The map's coast line names the wreck.
  - The lighthouse beam: at night, and in fog by day, the lamp turns every 14 seconds and sweeps the water for half of each turn. When it crosses a waiting float a fish comes at once, and glowing fish are three times likelier for that cast. The Beacon Herring (uncommon, night only, glows) is the one it's for; it also triples the Gaslight Angler's odds, the only other glowing coast fish, for the casts the beam crosses. The lamp burns in fog by day too, when the herring isn't about, so on a foggy day the beam only lifts the Angler (kept: fog is the coast's rarest weather, 18% of days, and the beam crosses a few casts in ten). A herring in the keepnet satisfies Wren's "a fish that glows" like a Lantern Carp, which is intended: she asks for anything that glows.
  - What the coast pays now (the simulator, steady player, Brasscap, coins an hour, noon and 11 PM): open water 11,200 and 12,400, the wash 18,400 and 17,300, the rocks 14,900 and 14,200, the kelp 16,100 and 15,100, the wreck 22,000 and 25,100, the trench 33,100 and 42,900, far water 27,100 and 31,500. So the trench and the far water stay the best, the wash is a quick spot for the Spindrift, and the wreck is where to go for treasure and the conger.
  - The simulator knows the coast now: the wash and the wreck in `SIM_SPOTS`, the seventh wave's harder hit in a fight, and a cast churned or lit as often as the swell's and the lamp's clocks line up with the wait, plus a share of casts a player times to them (`SIM_PLAYERS` seventh .1 and beam .2 steady, .03 and .08 new). A cast to the wash waits for a swell to break first and can't wash out.
  - The whole run now (11 runs, steady): the boat 2 h 09, the Beacon Herring 2 h 23, the Comber Tarpon 5 h 48, Deepwater 7 h 30, everything about 10 hours (median 9 h 52 in one set of 11 runs and 10 h 17 in another, single runs 8 to 11.5). Nothing was repriced. The whole-run report picks the best-paying spot three stretches in four and a random one otherwise, so it seldom fishes the wreck or the wash: the Spindrift comes in about half the runs and the conger in two to five of 11, and the pace table leaves both out. A player gets the wreck's tip on their first idle moment at the coast.

- **The Saltmarsh (build 27).** The third water, down the river from Wren's boathouse, reached in her punt. It sits between the river and the coast on the map's route, and pays about what the river does.
  - The way in is Wren's first quest: show her a fish that glows (a Lantern Carp from the lake, say), live in your keepnet, and her punt runs down to the marsh. Most players have one by the time they meet her, so in the simulator the marsh opens about five minutes after the ferry (1 h 08). It's a second water to choose, not a gate.
  - The tide is the marsh's mechanic. It runs 12.4 in-game hours from one high water to the next, so it turns about every six hours and comes about 48 minutes later each day, as a real tide does; the first high water is at 3 AM on day 0. How far it swings follows the moon: all the way at the spring tides (new and full moon), a little over half at the quarters (neap). The clock's icon shows the tide, and tapping it says when it turns; the Almanac has a tide line.
  - Four mud banks (the flat, the spit, the bar and the saltings) come up out of the water as the tide falls and shrink as it rises. A cast onto the mud goes splat and that's the cast; a float the falling tide leaves on the mud is stranded, and that's the cast too (with the Twin Spool, you fish on with the float that's still in the water). A tide pool is never mud: while the flood is reaching into it, it's open water (the flats) until the tide covers it. Each comes with a tip the first time. The saltings, sea lavender and samphire, are covered only by the high water of a spring tide.
  - Six spots, drawn rather than marked: open water, the reeds by the boardwalk, the flooded flats (a bank under water), the tide pools (on a bank while the mud round them is out), the sluice pool by the old sluice gate (deep) and the far water. A rising tide shortens the wait for a bite by 15% (the fish feed on the flood), and a tide pool by 20% (fish are trapped in it).
  - Nine new fish, more than the plan's four, so the tide and the weather always leave something to fish for: the Mudlark Eel and the Doormat Flounder (commons; the Mudlark comes up more at night), the Croaking Bass (uncommon, five times likelier from 5 to 9 PM), the Cucumber Smelt (rain), the Will-o'-Whiting (fog, glows: the marsh lights), the Thicklip Mullet (rare), the Bellmouth (Epic, comes up to the Drowned Bell in a pocket, in the rain, 1 in 14), the Lampwick Eel (Epic and secret, at night in the rain in the Lantern Rod's light, 1 in 12, in the marsh and at the lake: the second shared fish) and Old Reeve (Legendary, three times likelier at the spring tides, worth 1,200). The marsh is the foggiest water (fog 34% of days, against the lake's 15%).
  - There are no traps in the marsh (the tide would strand them), its fish live in the salt tank (brackish, but closer to the sea than the lake), which now opens with the marsh as well as the boat, since the marsh comes about an hour before the boat, and the kitchen has no marsh dishes yet: the Mudlark and Bellmouth meals come with the full roster (step 31).
  - Wren's second marsh quest: catch her a marsh light (a Will-o'-Whiting), and she gives you the Lantern Rod.   - What they bring: Smokehouse Kedgeree (a smoked fish through rice), then a better smoker (a fourth hook, and Delicacies in 6 hours instead of 8), a second burner (an order makes a portion for you too, to eat or save), a bigger spice rack (garlic and fennel seed: seven jars) with Harbor Pepperpot, and your uncle's ladle, a keepsake (meals last 25% longer).
  - Each new standing plays a short visit at the kitchen window, one line at a time, ending on its gift. Barnaby brings the burner; until you've met him, it comes across on Ottilie's ferry with your name chalked on the crate, so no one visits before you've met them.
- **Orders are safe for the fish and the save.** Fish a pinned order needs stay out of the sale when you land them and when a trap is hauled. Grey only takes a common you can spare: never a record, never one a ticket needs.
  - The fish in the pan are kept in the save until a cook is done, so a page closed mid-cook hands them back on the next start, and a meal that was cooked but not yet eaten or saved waits in the pantry.
  - Old saves that had cooked open orders at once. Ticket data from a save is checked against the tables: unknown people, recipes or twists are dropped, and what's written on a ticket is always one of that person's own lines.
- **Idle play arrives as fish traps, a smoke rack and a proper welcome back (build 19).** It covers the boring part, the common fish, and leaves everything interesting to active play.
  - Traps catch only commons and uncommons you've already caught, from the bite pool of the spot they sit on. They never discover a species, a rare or a story item.
  - Everything idle runs on real time, playing or not, and counts at most 8 hours at a stretch.
- **The 30% guardrail is measured in what idle earns, coins and Glimmer, as the design doc says ("offline progress is worth about 30% of active fishing"; "capped at 30% of active income").**
  - From the simulator, with traps collected every 2 hours and active play at a real player's half pace, traps earn 18% of active coins with the Willow Switch and 2–5% later on. Glimmer runs 8–24%.
  - In fish, a full lake set catches about 30 an hour, 26–31% of active play. Traps in both waters fill at once, so with both sets it's about 55 an hour, about half of active play, and three-quarters with Wide Mesh on every trap. That's fine, because they're cheap commons, they don't count toward mastery or records, and they sell for little. `npm run sim -- --idle` reports all three shares.
- **Traps, prices and rates.**
  - Your uncle's trap turns up on the dock at 25 catches.
  - Ottilie sells two more for the lake (300 and 900 coins) and three sea pots for the coast once you have a boat (1,500, 3,500 and 7,000).
  - A trap holds 20 and catches one fish every 6 minutes at the lake and every 7 at the coast, so an empty one is full in 2 to 2⅓ hours.
  - Each water has three marked spots, one trap to a spot. You set a trap with a tap or a drag from the dock or the deck.
- **Hauling a trap keeps the fish your known recipes still need, while the keepnet has room, and sells the rest.**
  - About one fish in five brings a speck of Glimmer.
  - The time toward the next fish carries over, so hauling often loses nothing.
- **Fittings are bought once and go on as many traps as you like, one to a trap.**
  - Bait Box (400): picks the common it goes for, six times as likely.
  - Wide Mesh (800): holds 40 and catches a fish in 0.7 of the time (every 4.2 minutes at the lake), but takes commons only. It's for a long night away.
  - Lantern Cage (1,500): night fish only, worth more, but each fish takes 1.6 times as long.
  - A fitting can't go on a trap that holds more than the fitting allows, so the sheet hauls it up first. No fish is ever lost.
- **The smoke rack has three hooks in the kitchen** (four from step 19, once the town makes you a regular).
  - A fish gains value on an ease-out curve: about +20% after an hour, and +60% at 6 hours.
  - A rare fish or rarer left 8 hours becomes a Hollowmere Delicacy, worth 2.5 times its fresh value.
  - A smoked fish goes back to the keepnet, or sells. It can't be cooked, go in a tank or be smoked again.
  - Per hook it's a small earner: a Saltjaw adds about 280 coins an hour as a Delicacy.
- **Coming back.**
  - Time away is measured from the last save, which happens every 30 seconds while the game is open (in the kitchen and aquarium too), and when it goes to the background or closes.
  - The in-game clock moves on at its usual pace, an in-game hour a real minute, after 2 minutes away.
  - After 20 minutes away, Grey waits by your fullest lake trap and a gull sits on your fullest sea pot, once one is 30% full. Hauling it up sends them off.
  - After 2 hours away there's Fresh water: rare fish and rarer bite twice as often for the next 5 casts. Like a meal's last cast, the fifth still counts until its line comes in.
- **A clock set backwards never pays twice.**
  - Real time never runs earlier than the last save, so after the clock goes back, traps, the rack and time away wait for real time to catch up. Setting it forward and back again earns nothing extra.
  - A last save more than a day ahead means a clock that was wrong and has been put right, so time starts over from now instead of waiting a day.
- **A tab only saves over storage it last read or wrote itself.**
  - If anything else has written the save since (another tab, or this one woken from a long sleep after missing the other tab's news), an ordinary save stops and shows "Open somewhere else" instead of putting an older game back.
  - Leaving the page saves the same way, and restoring a backup is the one deliberate overwrite.
- **On a short phone (360×640), trap floats never sit lower than 250 px above the bottom of the screen, and the lily-pads spot is left of the middle,** clear of Barnaby's boat. A tap goes to the townsfolk and the keepnet before a float.

- **Glimmer is the second currency (build 18).**
  - It comes from beating a personal record (2 Glimmer for a common fish up to 40 for a godly one), from Glimmer geodes, a new common treasure (4–8 at the lake, 6–12 at the coast), and from every crate (3–5 in a common one, up to 90–120 in a mythic one). Mutations add more in step 21.
  - Records pay Glimmer instead of coins.
- **Until Wren's bench (step 25), runes are etched with your uncle's etching kit in the tackle bag.**
  - Etching a rune costs Glimmer once. After that it goes on any rod for free, one of each per rod, so trying a build costs nothing.
  - Rods have 1 to 3 sockets, rising up the ladder: one on the Willow Switch and Reedcutter, two on the Ash Caster, Heronwood and Brasscap Pro, three on the sea rods.
  - The first six are Swift Spool, Magpie Knot, Lure of the Deep, Nightglass, Wanderer and Echo. Storm Knot comes with weather (step 20), Odd Water with mutations (step 21) and Homebody with Wren (step 25).
- **Wanderer counts each water separately (the lake, the coast), as the design doc's "each location" says,** over an in-game day of 24 minutes.
  - Every catch counts toward the day, rune or not, so etching it in mid-day doesn't pay twice. A fish the Hungry Hook eats doesn't count.
  - It pays a fixed number of catches a day, so it's worth more to a slower player, and to one who travels.
- **An Echo waits at the spot of the perfect hook, for the next cast there.** Casting elsewhere loses it. If treasure comes up first, the Echo keeps waiting, so Echo never costs a treasure roll.
- **Nightglass's catch is that fish bite more slowly by day,** not the doc's "day-only fish are rarer", because there are no day-only fish yet. Its "and fog fish" half arrives with weather in step 20.
- **Rune balance, from the simulator** (a steady player, 3,000–5,000 casts; Wanderer's figure is for a nonstop player who never travels):

  | Rune | What it does to a run |
  | --- | --- |
  | Lure of the Deep | 5–15% more coins an hour |
  | Swift Spool | About 6% more fish an hour |
  | Magpie Knot | Twice the treasure and about twice the Glimmer, coins about the same |
  | Echo | 5–10% more fish an hour, most of them the kind you just hooked |
  | Wanderer | 2–3% more coins an hour |
  | Nightglass | 35% more coins an hour in the trench at night, 9% fewer by day |

  - Glimmer runs about 70–130 an hour in the simulator, mostly from treasure. At a real player's pace, the first rune comes within the first hour, and all six take several hours.
- **The HUD fits by measuring, not by guessing from the screen width.** When something in the row would be cut off, it gives way a step at a time: tighter chips, then the clock drops AM and PM, then big numbers shorten (125K), then smaller text, and last, the Playtest button steps aside (a long press on the clock still opens Playtest). This also fixed the coin icon, which had been squeezed to nothing on narrow phones.
- **Tiles in cards and sheets are painted at their layout size, not their size on screen,** so a sheet mid-zoom no longer paints them at the wrong size. The catch card paints its rune badges once it's on screen.
- **The art pass got a second part for the aquarium and the kitchen (build 17),** at the owner's request. From now on the art bar covers every screen, overlays and icons included, not just the fishing scene.
  - The tank is sized to the space between the tabs and the buttons, so nothing overlaps on a short screen. Rooms measure their layout, not their on-screen size, because they open with a small zoom.
  - Decor icons in the shop and in crates are drawn from the tank's own drawings instead of separate SVGs, so the two can't drift apart.
  - Still parts of the art are painted once and copied each frame. In the same CPU-throttled test, the aquarium runs a little faster than build 16, and the kitchen measures the same.
  - The kitchen's herbs stay still, because making them sway cost more frame rate than it was worth. The plan's steaming kettle became a steaming mug of tea on the shelf, since a kettle on a shelf wouldn't steam.
- **After every step or major update, the roadmap doc and the current build are sent together in the chat.**
- **Scene art pass (build 16).**
  - The angler is drawn from behind: a mustard oilskin coat, a slate hat with a brass band and a fly, and both hands on the rod. They breathe, glance along the shore, and wind the reel while reeling.
  - The reel on the rod, and the line's color on the water, follow the tackle that's on it.
  - The dock, sea stacks, banks and skiff deck are painted once into cached layers. Under the same CPU throttling, the frame rate measured the same as build 15.
  - The water, sky, far shore and reeds were kept as they were.
- **The art bar applies from step 15, and step 16 is a scene art pass.**
  - The angler, the dock props, Ottilie's ferry and the coast's rocks and kelp were too plain beside the water and sky.
  - They're redone before more is built around the dock, so new pieces are made to the bar from the start.
  - The roadmap from step 16 on moved down one number.
- **The bite stat now covers the whole wait for a bite:** the quiet, the swim over and the nibbles, not just the quiet.
  - Bait scaled only the quiet, which is about a seventh of a cast, so worms made no measurable difference.
  - Now they bring about 7% more fish an hour.
  - The Old Cork Float, worms, chum and the Clearwater Line all read "Fish bite N% sooner".
- **Each rod keeps its own reel and line, and bait rides on whichever rod you hold.**
  - Swapping rods swaps their tackle, so a rod is a whole build.
  - Bait comes in tins of a set number of casts.
  - Switching bait keeps an opened tin's casts.
  - A tin's last cast still counts until that cast ends.
  - A lure never runs out.

## 2026-10-02

- **Treasure turns up about one cast in 12,** with crates about a quarter of it.
  - It adds 10–20% to coins an hour with a good rod.
  - About 15 new finds turn up in the first 1,000 casts.

  - You start with one pocket. Ottilie sews up to four more, for 1,500 to 45,000 coins (1,500 to 20,000 from build 25).
